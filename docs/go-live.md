# Going live: a note for the owner

**What it does.** Staff message the Money Desk on WhatsApp to record invoices coming in and going out, see
who owes what, chase late payers and pay suppliers. Paystack moves the money, and your Google Sheet is the
books.

**What it doesn't do yet.** It doesn't message customers itself: staff send each reminder with one tap. It
doesn't connect to accounting software or run payroll. It never approves its own payments.

**Before real customers use it**
- Switch Paystack to live keys. Your Paystack business must be registered, or transfers are blocked.
- Keep enough money in the Paystack balance to pay suppliers, or set settlement so collections stay there.
- Set your two limits: the most for one payment, and the most per day. Above either, a payment waits for you.
- Lock the Sheet. Only you should be able to tick `approved`. Give staff only the access they need. The
  checks catch honest mistakes, not someone with edit rights who means harm.
- Run a pilot for 2 weeks, with 5 households and 3 suppliers.

**Your one job: the tick.** New bank details, or a payment over a limit, waits until you tick `approved` on
that row. A chat message saying "oga said it's fine" never counts. The desk doesn't see the tick happen, so
staff message it again ("it's ticked, pay now") and the payment goes then.

**When things go wrong**
- A payment that fails or bounces back stays unpaid in the Sheet. The desk says so when it pays or checks.
- If the desk is down, the Sheet still works, and so does paying by hand.
- Mark cash payments yourself in the Sheet. They show as unverified, because Paystack has no record of them.
- Payments show up when someone asks for the cash position or checks an invoice, not the moment they land.

**Customer data.** The Sheet holds names and phone numbers, so Nigeria's Data Protection Act (2023)
applies. Limit who can open it, and remove people you no longer deal with.
